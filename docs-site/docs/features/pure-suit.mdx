# 清一色听牌 (Pure Suit Waits)

Use this hand assistant to analyze a one-suit hand (清一色), identify immediate winning tiles, compare discard choices, and account for the visible discard river.

Route: [`/#/pure-suit`](https://ubergoonz.github.io/mj-dw/#/pure-suit)

![清一色听牌 screenshot](/img/features/pure-suit.png)

## How it works

1. Choose the suit: 萬, 筒, or 索. The summary row shows claimed sets, concealed tiles, and the hand sizes for listening and discarding; a status line under it always tells you what to do next. `Reset` clears everything.
2. Step 1 · Select tiles: each of the nine suit tiles has two steppers — `Hand` for copies in your concealed hand and `River` for copies you can see in the discard river. The `N / 4 used` caption under each tile counts hand, claimed-set, and river copies together, so you can never record more than four copies of a tile.
3. Your hidden hand appears on the 14-slot rack below the selector. The red outline marks your latest draw, tap any rack tile to remove it, and enable `Auto-sort hand` to keep the rack ordered. Claimed sets lock three hidden-hand slots each; locked slots are shown disabled.
4. The Discard river rack lists every river tile you recorded, with the running count. Tap a river tile to remove it.
5. Step 2 · Claim / concealed set: record completed sets, grouped into Chi, Pong, and hidden Gang tabs:
	- Chi · from player: claim a three-tile sequence discarded by another player. Each sequence offers three buttons — one per permutation — so you can record exactly which tile you claimed (shown on the top row, with your two hand tiles below)
	- Pong · from player: claim the third matching tile discarded by another player (top row is the claimed tile, bottom row is your pair)
	- Gang · hidden: all four matching tiles are self-drawn
	Buttons that cannot be formed right now are greyed out with an explanatory tooltip. In the recorded set list, the claimed tile is tilted, matching how it is placed on a real table, and each set has a `Clear` button.
6. A claimed Pong card shows a `Self-draw → Gang` action after you draw its fourth matching tile. This records a gang upgraded from the earlier claim.
7. The **Ways to complete your hand** panel updates live at any hand size. It shows the best grouping of your concealed tiles (sets and the pair, with any loose tiles marked), then lists exactly which discards are worth calling: a `Pong` badge when a third copy of a pair in your hand is discarded, and a `Chi` badge with the specific hand tiles a discard would complete. The status line under the summary also names the top calls.
8. At the listening hand size, the Winning tiles panel lists every tile that completes a standard one-suit hand, with the remaining copy count for each and the overall known-suit draw chance.
9. At the discard hand size, Best discard choices ranks each viable discard by the chance of drawing one of its resulting waits from the known remaining tiles in the selected suit. The top option is highlighted, each resulting wait is shown as a tile image, and the `Discard to river` button on a suggestion moves that tile from your hand to the river in one step so the odds stay in sync with the table.

## Hand sizes

A standard winning hand has four sets and a pair. Completed exposed sets reduce the number of concealed tiles that need to be entered:

| Completed exposed sets | Listening hand size | Before-discard hand size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 13 | 14 |
| 1 | 10 | 11 |
| 2 | 7 | 8 |
| 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 4 | 1 | 2 |

## Odds note

The percentage is a **known-suit draw chance**, not a full wall probability. It subtracts only your entered hand and visible river tiles from the 36 tiles of the selected suit. Opponents' hidden hands, the wall, and the exact tiles in your exposed sets are not known to this calculator.
